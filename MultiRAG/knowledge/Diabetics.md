# Understanding Diabetes

## What is Diabetes?

Diabetes is a chronic medical condition that occurs when the body cannot
properly regulate blood sugar (glucose) levels. Glucose is the body's
primary source of energy and comes from the food we eat.

Insulin, a hormone produced by the pancreas, helps glucose enter cells
to be used for energy. When insulin production or function is impaired,
blood sugar levels rise.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Types of Diabetes

### 1. Type 1 Diabetes

-   Autoimmune condition
-   Body attacks insulin-producing cells
-   Requires lifelong insulin therapy
-   Often diagnosed in children and young adults

### 2. Type 2 Diabetes

-   Most common type
-   Body becomes resistant to insulin or doesn't produce enough
-   Linked to lifestyle and genetic factors
-   Can often be managed with lifestyle changes and medication

### 3. Gestational Diabetes

-   Occurs during pregnancy
-   Usually resolves after childbirth
-   Increases future risk of Type 2 diabetes

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Common Symptoms

-   Frequent urination
-   Increased thirst
-   Extreme hunger
-   Fatigue
-   Blurred vision
-   Slow-healing wounds
-   Unexplained weight loss (more common in Type 1)

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Risk Factors

-   Family history of diabetes
-   Overweight or obesity
-   Physical inactivity
-   Poor diet
-   Age (risk increases after 45 for Type 2)
-   High blood pressure
-   High cholesterol

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Complications (If Uncontrolled)

-   Heart disease
-   Stroke
-   Kidney disease
-   Nerve damage (neuropathy)
-   Vision problems (retinopathy)
-   Foot problems
-   Increased risk of infections

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Management & Prevention

### Lifestyle Changes

-   Regular physical activity (at least 150 minutes/week)
-   Balanced diet (high fiber, low refined sugar)
-   Weight management
-   Stress management
-   Adequate sleep

### Medical Management

-   Blood sugar monitoring
-   Oral medications (for Type 2)
-   Insulin therapy (if required)
-   Regular medical check-ups

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Healthy Eating Tips

-   Prefer whole grains over refined carbs
-   Include vegetables in every meal
-   Choose lean proteins
-   Limit sugary beverages
-   Control portion sizes

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## When to See a Doctor

-   Persistent symptoms of high blood sugar
-   Very high or very low blood sugar readings
-   Signs of complications (vision changes, numbness, chest pain)

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Conclusion

Diabetes is a manageable condition with proper medical care, lifestyle
adjustments, and regular monitoring. Early diagnosis and consistent
management significantly reduce the risk of complications.

For personalized advice, always consult a qualified healthcare
professional.
